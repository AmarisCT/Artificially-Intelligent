Consciousness and attention

Attention and consciousness seem to go together: what you attend to is usually what you are aware of. This lesson asks whether they are the same thing, and why the answer matters for theories.

Learning objectives

  • Define attention in a way that differs from awareness.
  • Describe why researchers debate whether attention and consciousness can come apart.
  • Explain how a theory can make attention central to awareness.

Spotlight and awareness

Attention is the selection of some information for fuller processing at the expense of other information. You cannot read this sentence and also follow every conversation in the room. Awareness seems to track attention closely, which is why the two are easy to confuse.

The researchers Christof Koch and Naotsugu Tsuchiya reviewed evidence that they are different processes that can sometimes be separated [1]. Their argument is that there are cases where attention appears to be present without awareness, and cases where awareness appears to occur with little attention. Both kinds of case are contested, and the interpretation of such experiments is a live topic.

Why the distinction matters

If attention and awareness are the same, a theory of attention would explain consciousness. If they can come apart, then explaining how attention works is not enough, and we need a further account of what makes some attended or unattended contents conscious.

This is why some theories treat the relationship so carefully. The Global Neuronal Workspace Theory describes consciousness in terms of information being amplified and made widely available, a process in which attention is closely involved [2]. Attention Schema Theory takes a different route: it proposes that the brain builds a simplified model of its own attention, and that what we call awareness is the content of that model [3]. These are different claims and they have different predictions.

Keeping the claim small

When you read about attention and awareness, check which claim is made. 'Attention and awareness usually go together' is uncontroversial. 'Awareness requires attention' is disputed. 'Awareness is attention' is a stronger claim still. The evidence for each is different.

A worked example: the crowded room

At a busy gathering you can follow one conversation and ignore the others. If someone across the room says your name, you may notice it even though you were not attending to that voice. One reading is that attention filters, but some signals are strong enough to break through. Another is that you were aware of the other voices all along, faintly. A third is that part of the processing happened without awareness and what you noticed was the result. These readings predict different things, and experiments try to tell them apart. The lesson's point is that an ordinary experience can be described in more than one way, and that the choice affects what you think attention and awareness are.

Common misunderstandings

  • “What you attend to is what you are conscious of, so they are the same thing.” They usually go together. Whether they can be separated is debated, and the evidence on both sides is contested.
  • “Attention schema theory says attention does not exist.” The theory proposes that awareness is the brain's simplified model of attention. Attention itself, as a process of selecting information, is not denied.

Reflection question

Recall a time when your attention was drawn away mid-conversation. What, if anything, were you still aware of?
